A timeline of what archaeology, inscriptions and ancient writers tell us about the people of the Iranian plateau and its borderlands: the Zagros, Susiana, the Caspian shore, Sistan and Kerman. Reading the dates

Years ago / BP is used for deep prehistory (“before present”, conventionally 1950). Subtract about 2,000 to convert to BCE: 12,000 years ago ≈ 10,000 BCE. cal means a radiocarbon date calibrated to calendar years. Palaeolithic dates often come with wide error ranges and sometimes conflict between methods, as at Kaldar Cave.

For the Median and early Achaemenid periods much of the narrative comes from Greek writers, especially Herodotus, writing a century or more later. Assyrian and Babylonian records and Persian royal inscriptions are closer to the events but tell only part of the story, so where they disagree the entry is marked debated. Map pins are placed at the best-known site for each entry; empire-wide or climatic events are pinned to a representative location.
